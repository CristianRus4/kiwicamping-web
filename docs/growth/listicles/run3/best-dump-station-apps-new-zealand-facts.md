## 2. Official facts for the short rules section

- **Who can freedom camp and the waste rule.** Since 7 June 2026 only a green-warrant vehicle counts as self-contained; "Vehicles with the old blue warrant certification cannot be used for freedom camping." Transition ran "until 6 June 2026". The requirements "do not apply" at "a campground, holiday park, or on private property". Certification levy $120 incl GST. https://www.pgdb.co.nz/self_contained_vehicles/private_vehicle_owners/ . (A search summary gave 7 June 2025: outdated, ignore.)
- **What the regulations say about emptying.** Plumbers, Gasfitters, and Drainlayers (Self-Contained Vehicles) Regulations 2023 (SL 2023/217, version as at 29 August 2024), reg 13: eight requirements including "(b) a fixed toilet", "(c) a wastewater system that both stores and collects greywater and blackwater", "(f) a means of disposing of wastewater hygienically to an appropriate outfall", "(g) secure rubbish storage". Reg 14: facilities must "operate for a minimum of 3 days for the maximum number of occupants". https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2023/217/en/latest/ . The regulations do not mention NZS 5465 (searched text).
- **NZS 5465:2001.** NZMCA calls it the "NZ Standard for Self Containment of Motor Caravans and Caravans" and says its Appendix B sets minimum dump-station installation requirements; NZMCA says dump stations "are typically built in accordance with NZ Standard 5465:2001". NZMCA's guide quotes B2 and B3: un-floodable level site, good vehicle access, a self-cleansing unit "of at least 600 x 500 mm" with an upstand of at least 100 mm and a gradient of at least 1 in 60. I have not read the standard itself (paid): unverified beyond NZMCA's quotation. Safe wording: "NZMCA says public dump stations are typically built to the NZS 5465:2001 design; vehicle certification now runs under the 2023 regulations." Sources: https://www.nzmca.org.nz/dump-stations and NZMCA Dump Station Guide (August 2015) https://cdn.nzmca.org.nz/production/pages/hL5lKNU3eDfZrLAJv4J0oeiPMkm5R0i2ZHyPf9G7.pdf
- **NZMCA funding conditions for public dump stations (2015 guide, may have changed):** "Accessible to the general public 24 hrs a day" and "Able to be used for no charge (We are not opposed to a donation so long as the donation is not 'required')". Supports "NZMCA-assisted public dump stations are meant to be free"; not a rule for every dump station.
- **Fine.** Freedom Camping (Penalties for Infringement Offences) Regulations 2023 (SL 2023/136): "depositing waste in or on the area while freedom camping" $800 infringement fee, $2,400 court maximum (council land s 20(1)(e); conservation land s 20A(c); LINZ land s 20B(d)). https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2023/136/en/latest/
- **Council examples (page read, fees vary).** Waitaki: free at north Oamaru, east Omarama and Mill Reserve (Palmerston); at lakes camping grounds "free if you have purchased a camping ground ticket", otherwise "$5 for using a dump station" via donation box (https://waitaki.govt.nz/our-facilities/camping/Pages/caravanandcampervandumpstations.aspx). Hurunui lists dump stations at Amberley Beach Camping Ground (South), Cheviot, Culverden, Hanmer Springs and Waikari (text says "four" but lists five: conflict) and says private camping grounds and petrol stations also have them, "best to have a look on the Camper Mate App or AA map" (https://www.hurunui.govt.nz/recreation/campervancaravan-dump-stations). Far North, Selwyn, Thames-Coromandel, Nelson council pages blocked my fetch (403/404); only search excerpts seen, unverified: do not quote their fees.
- **DOC.** The camping-responsibly page says only "Start with clean gear, take all rubbish with you and use toilets where provided"; no dump-station list found on DOC pages read. https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/know-before-you-go/camping-responsibly/
