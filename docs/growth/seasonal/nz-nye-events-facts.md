# NZ New Year's Eve and summer event camping 2026-27: official-source facts

Research lane for the guide "New Year's Eve and summer event camping in New Zealand" (campingapp.nz).

- Date read for everything below: 2026-10-21 unless a row says otherwise.
- Method: pages fetched with curl or WebFetch and read; quotes copied from fetched page text. Rows marked **SEARCH EXTRACT** come only from a web-search summary because the page could not be read; the wording there is what the search tool returned, not text I read on the page. Treat those as unverified.
- "not published" = the official source was read and is silent, or no official page was found.
- DOC, green warrant, fire-season and public-holiday research is NOT repeated here. See `nz-christmas-facts.md` in this folder (sections 1 to 5) and cross-reference it.
- Sites that blocked automated access are listed in section 8 (not circumvented).
- Status: complete for the brief; see section 9 for hand checks.

## 0. Read first: things that change the guide

1. **Mount Maunganui Beachside Holiday Park is closed** after the Mauao landslide of 22 January 2026 (six lives lost, per Tauranga City Council's FAQ). No bookings, no waitlist, no reopening timeframe. Handle with care and do not recommend it. Section 1.2.
2. **Rhythm and Vines 2026 (28 to 31 December, Gisborne) is billed as the final festival at Waiohika Estate**; it moves to an undisclosed North Island site in 2027. Section 3.1.
3. **Thames-Coromandel Christmas/New Year liquor ban runs 4pm 23 December to 4pm 6 January** (6 January 2027 is a Wednesday, so no weekend extension). But the bylaw exempts alcohol drunk inside a certified self-contained vehicle at a designated freedom camping area. Section 1.1.
4. **Waikato District (Raglan) seasonal alcohol ban was extended in 2025 to 23 December to 10 February**; freedom camping on James Street, Raglan is banned. Section 1.5.
5. **The under-20 drink-driving limit is zero, not 150 mcg.** Adult limit is 250 mcg breath. Section 5.1.
6. **Marlborough Wine and Food Festival (Feb 2027) is on hold; Bay Dreams has no 2026 or 2027 edition confirmed; Beyond The Valley is an Australian festival, not NZ; Electric Avenue is a February Christchurch day festival with no camping.** Do not feature these as NYE camping events. Section 3.
7. **Kaikoura is in a flood recovery phase (July 2026 event)** per the council banner. Section 1.7.

### 1.1 Thames-Coromandel District Council (Whangamata, Whitianga, Pauanui, Tairua, Hahei, Hot Water Beach)

TCDC's site returns HTTP 403 to curl, but WebFetch could read several TCDC pages normally. The bylaw PDF itself was too large for WebFetch (over 10 MB) and 403 to curl, so the Schedule 1 maps were not read.

**Alcohol Control Bylaw 2018 (last amended 19 November 2019)**
- **Fact:** The Christmas/New Year holiday ban runs from 4pm on 23 December to 4pm on 6 January, applies "covering all places indicated on the maps" (Schedule 1 maps for Coromandel, Mercury Bay, Tairua-Pauanui, Thames and Whangamata); if 6 January is a Friday, Saturday or Sunday it extends to 4am the following Monday.
  - URL: https://www.tcdc.govt.nz/alcoholcontrol
  - Date read: 2026-10-21
  - Quote: "From 4pm on 23 December to 4pm on 6 January each year" and "extended to 4am on the following Monday."
  - 2026-27 note: 6 January 2027 is a Wednesday (calendar check), so the standard 4pm end applies.
- **Fact:** Other holiday ban periods: Auckland Anniversary, King's Birthday and Labour weekends 4pm Friday to 4pm Monday; Easter 4pm Thursday to 4pm Tuesday. Permanent bans run 24/7 in mapped areas.
  - Same URL. Quote: "24 hours a day, seven days a week, 365 days a year."
- **Fact:** Penalties in the bylaw (clause 12): infringement fee up to $1,000, court fine up to $20,000. The bylaw is made under the Local Government Act 2002 (clause 2.1).
  - Quote: "a person found in breach of the Bylaw is liable for an infringement fee not exceeding $1,000." and "a fine not exceeding $20,000."
  - Caution: the $1,000 is a ceiling in the bylaw. The fee actually charged by TCDC: not published on the pages read. (Tauranga says $250, see 1.2; one SEARCH EXTRACT also says $250 for Coromandel, unverified.)
- **Fact (important for campers):** Clause 7.3 exempts certified self-contained vehicles from the permanent and holiday bans when alcohol is consumed inside the vehicle, at a designated freedom camping area, within the occupant number on the certificate.
  - Same URL. Quote: "Clauses 6 and 9 do not apply to self-contained vehicles when: a) Alcohol is being consumed inside the self-contained vehicle; and b) It is being used for freedom camping in a designated freedom camping area; and c) does not exceed the maximum number of occupants stipulated on the vehicle's self-containment certificate".
  - Not an exemption: campgrounds, private land and licensed premises are not listed as exceptions in the bylaw text as read ("Licensed premises" appears only in definitions). Campground/holiday-park rules are set by the operator: not published by TCDC.
- **Fact:** Temporary bans need 7 days notice; the council ran one for Whangamata Beach Hop (4pm Tuesday 24 March 2026 to 4am Monday 30 March 2026). A temporary New Year-specific ban for 2026-27: not published (none announced as of 2026-10-21).
  - Quote: "The Council will give the public at least 7 days notice of any temporary ban."
- **Fact (TCDC alcohol bans page):** Holiday ban areas apply "during certain holiday periods"; maps cover Coromandel, Mercury Bay, Tairua-Pauanui, Thames, Whangamata. URL: https://www.tcdc.govt.nz/Our-Services/Licenses-Permits-and-Concessions/Alcohol-Licences/Alcohol-Bans (read 2026-10-21). Quote: "Holiday ban areas" apply "during certain holiday periods, for certain days and times."
- **SEARCH EXTRACT (not confirmed on a page I read):** the seasonal ban covers beaches and seaside reserves from Whangapoua to Whangamata and all of Tairua, Pauanui and Whangamata townships (derived from a 2018 council release on Scoop: https://scoop.co.nz/stories/AK1812/S00459/alcohol-and-fire-bans-begin-this-week.htm). Per-town exact boundaries: check the Schedule 1 maps by hand.
- **Police powers in the Whangamata ban:** Police statement 22 October 2024 (https://police.govt.nz/node/78647, read 2026-10-21): "several powers to search without warrant for the purposes of establishing the presence of alcohol" including "searching vehicles that are in or entering the alcohol ban area." URL: https://www.police.govt.nz/news/release/whangamata-police-encourage-everyone-celebrate-safely-new-years-eve (read 2026-10-21). Old; use only as colour, not as a current rule.

**Freedom Camping Bylaw 2022 (TCDC), read via WebFetch**
- **Fact:** Freedom camping needs a self-contained vehicle, max two consecutive nights, only between 5pm and 9am, prohibited in Schedule 1 areas, restricted in Schedule 2 areas (designated spots, vehicle caps, set leave days); maps for Coromandel-Colville, Mercury Bay, Tairua-Pauanui, Thames, Whangamata; approval to camp in a prohibited area needs an application at least 30 days ahead. ## 4. Liquor bans and the law

### 4.1 What a liquor ban (alcohol ban) is

- **Fact:** Territorial authorities can make bylaws banning, regulating or controlling the consumption, bringing in and possession of alcohol in public places, "either generally or for one or more specified periods", and, with that, alcohol in vehicles in public places. "Public place" means a place open to or being used by the public "whether free or on payment of a charge", excluding licensed premises. Unopened containers being transported home or to adjoining licensed premises are exempt.
  - URL: https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2002/0084/latest/DLM172980.html (Local Government Act 2002, s 147; consolidated Act "as at 01 October 2026" on the fetched copy)
  - Date read: 2026-10-21
  - Quote: "A territorial authority may make bylaws for its district for the purpose of prohibiting or otherwise regulating or controlling, either generally or for one or more specified periods, any or all of the following: (a) the consumption of alcohol in public places: (b) the bringing of alcohol into public places: (c) the possession of alcohol in public places." and "the presence or consumption of alcohol in vehicles, or vehicles of stated kinds or descriptions, in public places."
- **Fact:** Police powers (s 169): a constable may without warrant search a container or a vehicle in or entering a restricted place, seize alcohol and its container, arrest a person found committing an offence, and arrest anyone who refuses to leave or surrender alcohol; seized alcohol is forfeited to the Crown if the person pays the infringement fee. The infringement fee amount comes from regulations; councils quote their own figure ($250 in Tauranga).
  - URL: https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2002/0084/latest/DLM173433.html (read via the same consolidated Act page)
  - Date read: 2026-10-21
  - Quote: "A constable may, without warrant, ... seize and remove any alcohol (and its container) that is in a restricted place in breach of an alcohol ban" and "arrest any person whom the constable finds committing an offence".
  - Search powers apply only where the bylaw authorises them (s 170, SEARCH EXTRACT summary of the Act, not read line by line).
- **Fact (council wording, Tauranga):** "Police may search and seize, without warrant, any container ... as well as any vehicle that is in, or entering, an alcohol-free area." (1.2)
- **Penalty figure:** The amount is the infringement fee set by regulations. Tauranga says $250. TCDC's bylaw says up to $1,000 infringement / $20,000 court fine (a ceiling) and the fee charged: not published. Taupo, Waikato, QLDC fee: not published on pages read.
- **Do liquor bans apply in freedom camping sites?** Yes if the freedom camping site is a public place inside a mapped ban area, unless the bylaw has a camper exemption. Official examples: Thames-Coromandel exempts alcohol drunk inside a certified self-contained vehicle at a designated freedom camping area (1.1, clause 7.3); Taupo allows drinking in lawfully parked certified self-contained vehicles except at Hipapatua Reserve (Reid's Farm) (1.6); Waikato exempts self-contained vehicles with conditions, except James Street Raglan (1.5); Tauranga says drinking in zones counts "including inside vehicles" with no camper exemption stated (1.2); Kaikoura's bylaw bans consuming alcohol in a vehicle in a ban area and lists "camp areas" inside the mapped streets (1.7). Always check the specific bylaw and map.
- **Do liquor bans apply in holiday parks and council campgrounds?** Depends on the map and the bylaw; not published as a general rule. TCDC's bylaw lists no campground exemption. Kaikoura's schedule includes "camp areas within the above specified areas".
- **DOC campsites:** DOC's freedom camping page, booking page, campervan page and low-impact camping page contain no alcohol rule (grep of read text, 2026-10-21): a national DOC campsite alcohol rule is not published. Campsite-specific rules exist: Dickey Flat (Kaimai Mamaku): "A permanent year-round liquor ban is in place in Dickey Flat. Keep liquor away from the site, including in vehicles." (https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/places-to-go/bay-of-plenty/places/kaimai-mamaku-conservation-park/things-to-do/dickey-flat-campsite/, read 2026-10-21; a SEARCH EXTRACT says it was closed after January 2026 storms, current status not read). Totaranui camp rules: "Disorderly behaviour, excessive alcohol consumption, or intoxication to the extent that other campers" are affected is not allowed; "Camp management reserves the right to search vehicles and campsites for fireworks, alcohol, and drugs." (camp-rules page in 2.1.)
- **Sale and Supply of Alcohol Act 2012:** referred to by the LGA for the definitions of "alcohol" and "licensed premises"; the Act itself was not read for this lane. Festivals' R18 and no-BYO conditions come from their liquor licences ("Due to our licence, we cannot allow BYO alcohol on site", R&A experience page https://rhythmandalps.co.nz/experience/).

## 5. Safety at New Year

### 5.1 Drink-driving limits (the brief's "150 mcg under 20" is wrong)

- **Fact:** Limit for drivers 20 and over: 250 micrograms of alcohol per litre of breath, or 50 mg per 100 ml of blood. Under 20: zero. A breath test above 250 mcg leads to an evidential breath test and "you will likely be forbidden to drive for 12 hours".
  - URL: https://www.police.govt.nz/advice-services/driving-and-road-safety/being-safe-road-rules-and-reasons
  - Date read: 2026-10-21
  - Quote: "aged 20 and over is: 250 micrograms of alcohol per litre of breath (mcg). The blood alcohol limit is 50 milligrams of alcohol per 100 millilitres of blood (mg). aged under 20 is: The limit is zero."
  - NZTA's page (https://www.nzta.govt.nz/safety/driving-safely/alcohol-and-drugs/) is blocked (Incapsula), so not read. Penalty amounts for exceeding the limit: not published on the Police page read.
