## 0. Things that may change the guide (read first)

1. Blue warrants stopped counting on 7 June 2026 (legislation, DOC and Responsible Camping agree). Fixed toilet is mandatory. See section 3.
2. The $1,000 figure is the legal ceiling for an infringement fee, not the usual fine. The fees actually set are $200, $400, $600 and $800. See section 3.
3. Tongariro Northern Circuit: DOC's own track page and a DOC media release disagree (track page says the Whakapapa to Mangatepopo section is not available for 2026/27, media release of 4 Sept says Mangatepopo Track reopens mid-December and Oturere Hut/campsite bookings open 18 December 2026). See section 2.
4. A very strong El Niño is forecast to peak in late spring or early summer; Earth Sciences NZ and FENZ both flag higher wildfire risk and earlier fire restrictions are possible. See section 4.
5. DOC Great Walk huts have no price change for 2026/27, but DOC's own hut/campsite price page for other facilities has new summer and "peak" (1 Dec to 29 Feb) tiers. See section 1.
6. The Act the user called "Self-contained Vehicles Legislation Act 2023" is actually titled "Self-contained Motor Vehicles Legislation Act 2023". Use the correct title in the guide.

## 1. DOC campsites: bookings and peak-season rules

### 1.1 Which campsites need booking in summer

- **Fact:** Bookings are required for all Serviced campsites and for some Scenic and Standard campsites in peak season; DOC says peak season is usually 1 October to 30 April, and each campsite page says whether it is bookable.
- URL: https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/places-to-stay/stay-at-a-campsite/booking-and-paying/
- Date read: 2026-10-21
- Quote: "Bookings are required for: all serviced campsites, and some scenic and standard campsites in peak season. Peak season is usually 1 October to 30 April."
- Note: this page still lists a "scenic" category. The facilities-and-fees page (1.4) lists Serviced, Standard, Basic, Backcountry and Great Walk campsites only, with no Scenic row. Do not give Scenic fees.

### 1.2 Non-bookable campsites are first come, first served

- **Fact:** Some DOC campsites cannot be booked and work first come, first served; some of those still charge a fee paid at a self-registration stand.
- URL: https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/places-to-stay/stay-at-a-campsite/booking-and-paying/
- Date read: 2026-10-21
- Quote: "Some campsites do not need to be booked. These campsites operate on a first come, first served basis."

### 1.3 Bookings are open, how far ahead, online vs phone, no waiting lists, no ballot

- **Fact:** DOC bookings for Great Walks, huts, campsites and lodges are open now for stays up to 30 June 2027; all bookings are first come first served and there are no waiting lists.

## 3. Freedom camping, self-containment and fines

### 3.1 Blue warrants stopped counting; green warrant required (7 June 2026)

- **Fact:** The transitional period under the Self-contained Motor Vehicles Legislation Act 2023 let privately owned vehicles use old (blue warrant) certification until the end date of period 4, which regulations set at 7 June 2026.
- URL (primary): https://www.legislation.govt.nz/regulation/public/2025/0067/latest/whole.html (Freedom Camping (Self-contained Motor Vehicles, Extension of Period 4) Regulations 2025, SL 2025/67; the official title uses a dash between "Vehicles" and "Extension")
- Date read: 2026-10-21
- Quote: "The end date of period 4 is 7 June 2026." and "These regulations come into force on 15 May 2025."
- **Fact (DOC):** From 7 June 2026 all vehicles must have a green warrant and a warrant card must be displayed in self-contained-only areas; vehicles with a portable toilet can no longer be certified.
- URL: https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/places-to-stay/stay-at-a-campsite/freedom-camping/
- Date read: 2026-10-21
- Quote: "From 7 June 2026, all vehicles must have a ‘green warrant’ to be certified self-contained under the new requirements. From this time, a warrant card must be displayed in your vehicle when camping in areas restricted to self-contained vehicles." and "Vehicles with a portable toilet can no longer be certified as self-contained. The toilet must be fixed to the floor of the vehicle."
- **Fact (Responsible Camping NZ, camping.org.nz):** Blue warrants are no longer valid; certification lasts four years and covers water supply, fixed toilet, wastewater, ventilation, sink and rubbish storage.
- URL: https://www.camping.org.nz/plan-your-trip/self-containment-certification
- Date read: 2026-10-21
- Quote: "Green warrants have replaced Blue warrants which are now no longer valid" and "To receive a Green warrant, your vehicle must meet requirements in relation to its water supply system, fixed toilet, wastewater system, ventilation system, sink, and rubbish storage." and "This certification will be valid for four years."
- Secondary (news, not official): 1News, 3 June 2026, https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/06/03/vehicle-camping-warrants-are-changing-what-you-need-to-know/ (read 2026-10-21). Quote: "From Sunday, June 7, blue warrants will no longer be recognised for freedom camping purposes." and "More than 68,000 vehicles have now been certified and issued with green self-containment warrants."
- Detail to double-check: the regulation says the period 4 "end date" is 7 June 2026; DOC and the news say recognition ends "from 7 June". Whether 7 June itself still counted is ambiguous. Irrelevant for summer 2026/27 but do not state the exact last day.
- Legal meaning of "self-contained": Freedom Camping Act 2011 definition (https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2011/61/en/latest/, read via the 2023 Act at https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2023/24/en/latest/, 2026-10-21). Quote: "self-contained, in relation to a motor vehicle, means that the vehicle has a valid certificate of self-containment issued in accordance with section 87U(3)(d) of the Plumbers, Gasfitters, and Drainlayers Act 2006".
- Register and certifiers: Plumbers, Gasfitters and Drainlayers Board runs the system (1News: "regulated the self-contained vehicle certification system and appointed certification authorities"; Responsible Camping page: "Visit the Register to find a Certification Authority operating in your region.").
- Official MBIE freedom camping pages (mbie.govt.nz/freedomcamping): blocked, see section 7.

### 3.2 Where only certified self-contained vehicles may camp

- **Fact:** Under DOC and council rules, many freedom camping places are marked "self-contained vehicles only"; tents are generally not allowed there; DOC lists prohibited and restricted places; freedom camping is not allowed at "No camping" signs.
- URL: https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/places-to-stay/stay-at-a-campsite/freedom-camping/
- Date read: 2026-10-21
- Quote: "Self-contained vehicles only sign. This sign means that only certified self-contained vehicles may camp overnight." and "Freedom camping is not allowed in areas where DOC has explicitly prohibited it, even if you are in a certified self-contained vehicle." and "Freedom camping is generally not permitted near Great Walks tracks."
- DOC rule for all freedom campers on conservation land: "light no fires."
- Legal definition of freedom camping (same page): "within 200 m of an area accessible by motor vehicle; within 200 m of the mean low water spring line of any sea or harbour; on or within 200 m of formed road or a Great Walks Track."

### 3.3 Infringement fees and fines

- **Fact:** The Freedom Camping Act caps an infringement fee at $1,000, defaults to $400 if no regulation sets it, and allows a court fine up to $3,000. The Penalties Regulations 2023 set the fees actually charged: $400 for camping in a non-self-contained vehicle where not permitted or in breach of a bylaw, $200 for failing to display a warrant card, $800 for damaging land or dumping waste, $600 for refusing to leave.
- URLs: Act s 20E https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2023/24/en/latest/ (consolidated Freedom Camping Act text inside); Regulations https://www.legislation.govt.nz/regulation/public/2023/0136/latest/whole.html
- Date read: 2026-10-21
- Quote (Act s 20E): "an infringement fee not exceeding $1,000 that is prescribed by regulations made under section 43; or if no regulations have been made under section 43 prescribing infringement fees, an infringement fee of $400; or a fine imposed by a court not exceeding $3,000".
- Quote (Regulations, Schedule 2): "Freedom camping in local authority area using a motor vehicle that is not self-contained, other than where permitted | 400 | 1,200"; "Failing to display a warrant card ... | 200 | 600"; "Interfering with or damaging a local authority area, its flora or fauna ... or depositing waste ... | 800 | 2,400". (The second number is the maximum fine.)
- Same schedule for conservation land (s 20A): $400 breach of notice, $200 no warrant card, $800 damage or waste, $600 refusing to leave.
- Tauranga City Council confirms the range for its area: "Infringement notices can vary in fee amounts from $400 to $1200, which are set by the New Zealand Government." (page in 3.4)
- The $1,000 in the brief is therefore the statutory ceiling for an infringement fee, not a fee actually in the schedule. Do not say "$1,000 fine" in the guide without that nuance.
- Fee figures read from the 2023 regulations "as made"; if MBIE has since raised fees, the MBIE page (blocked) would show it. Check by hand.

## 4. Fire seasons, fire rules, gas and barbecues

### 4.1 How fire seasons work (law and where to check)

- **Fact:** Fire and Emergency NZ can declare a prohibited or restricted fire season for any area by public notice; an area with neither is in an "open season". Lighting a fire in the open air in a prohibited season is an offence, with a penalty for individuals up to 2 years in prison or a fine up to $300,000 (the same maximum applies in a restricted season).
- URL: https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2017/17/en/latest/ (Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017, ss 56 to 58)
- Date read: 2026-10-21
- Quote (s 56): "FENZ may, by public notice, declare, for any area, a prohibited fire season: a restricted fire season. An area that is not in a prohibited or restricted fire season is in an open season."
- Quote (s 57): "A person must not light or allow another person to light a fire in open air in an area that is in a prohibited fire season." and "A person who commits an offence under this section is liable on conviction, in the case of an individual, to a term of imprisonment not exceeding 2 years or to a fine not exceeding $300,000, or both". Offence requires: "knowingly or recklessly contravenes".
- Permit exception: "a permit granted under regulations made under section 190".
- Official checker: Check It's Alright (checkitsalright.nz). The DOC page says to use "the Can I light a fire? tool" and choose "Campfires". The site is JavaScript only ("Browser or device not supported" to my fetch), so live status was not read. Status for any area on 2026-10-21: not read. See section 7.

### 4.2 DOC rules on conservation land

- **Fact:** DOC land has a restricted fire season all year; fires at campsites only in designated fireplaces and only when there is no fire ban; during a prohibited season those facilities are not available; gas burners and enclosed liquid fuel stoves can usually be used any time; portable solid-fuel stoves are not permitted.
- URL: https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/know-before-you-go/fires/
- Date read: 2026-10-21
- Quote: "On public conservation land there is a restricted fire season in place all year round." and "At campsites, only light fires in designated fireplaces and when there is no fire ban." and "During a prohibited fire season (fire ban)" fires are not allowed, and "On public conservation land you can usually use gas burners and enclosed liquid fuel stoves for cooking any time." and "Portable fireplaces or stoves using solid fuel such as wood, pellets, charcoal or coal are not permitted on public conservation land."
- Charcoal and umu: "you cannot light any other types of fire other than those specified in the Authorised Fire Types (including bonfires, charcoal bbqs, umu/hangi fires, and fireworks) unless you have a permit from FENZ. Permits in these cases are usually not granted."
- Site-specific bans: Papatowai "Fires are not allowed at any time"; Kauaeranga and Northern Coromandel campgrounds "total ban on open fires and fireworks" year round, "Only gas cookers, fridge/freezers and lanterns may be used."
- Backcountry fires: allowed only if no fire ban, 3 m clearance, under 0.5 m wide and high (same DOC fires page), not in the guide's main scope.
- Gas stoves and BBQs in a prohibited season on private land: official FENZ page blocked. SEARCH EXTRACT (FENZ fire plans summarised by the search tool): manufactured gas appliances such as barbecues "don't need to get a fire permit" in a prohibited season, but local notices differ (Far North notice says "only gas barbeques can be used"; Hawke's Bay "only gas and charcoal barbeques"). Not verified on the page; check checkitsalright.nz.
- Fines/infringement fees for a prohibited-season fire (as opposed to court penalty): not published in the sources I could read. The court maximum is in 4.1.
